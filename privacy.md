# GroceryDate Privacy Policy

**Last updated: 31 August 2026**

GroceryDate is made by one person. This policy describes what the app does with
your information, in the same plain terms the app itself uses.

---

## The short version

- Your photographs, item names and reminder dates stay **on your phone**.
- There is **no account**, no sign-in, and nothing that identifies you or your
  device.
- There is **no analytics, no advertising, no tracking** and no third-party SDK
  that reports back.
- Reading the date happens **on the device**. No photograph is uploaded to read
  it.
- There is exactly **one** way anything leaves your phone, it is **off by
  default**, and it asks before the first time. It is described in full below.

---

## What the app stores, and where

Everything below is kept in GroceryDate's own private storage on your device.
Other apps cannot read it, and it is removed when you uninstall the app.

| What | Why |
|---|---|
| The photograph of each item | Shown in the app and on the reminder notification |
| The item name you type | So you know what the reminder is about |
| The expiry date and reminder lead time | To schedule the reminder |
| Your settings | Language, default lead time, shutter volume, flash, and whether misread reporting is on |
| Diagnostic files from the most recent scan | To explain a misreading; overwritten by the next photo |

The app does **not** collect your contacts, your location, your device
identifiers, your advertising ID, your browsing, or anything about how you use
the app.

## Your calendar

If you allow calendar access, GroceryDate adds **one event per item** to your
default calendar, and deletes that event when you delete the item. It writes its
own events only. It does not read your other events, and it never sends any
calendar data anywhere.

Calendar access is optional. Decline it and the app still tracks your items and
still sends you reminders.

## Notifications

Reminders are **local notifications**, scheduled on your phone by your phone.
No server is involved, and nothing about a reminder is transmitted.

## The one exception: "Help improve scanning"

The app sometimes reads a date wrong. Fixing that needs the photograph that
defeated it, so **Settings → Help improve scanning** lets you send those
specific photographs to the developer.

**It is off until you turn it on.** If it has never been switched on, nothing
has ever left your phone.

### When you are asked

The first time a report would be sent — either because you corrected a date the
app had read, or because you pressed **Report misread date** — the app asks
whether you're willing to share it. You are asked **once, ever**. Declining is
remembered and is permanent unless you change it yourself in Settings.

### What is sent

Only for the specific photographs involved in a misreading:

- **The photograph** of the label.
- **The text the app read** from it, and its notes about how it read it.
- **The date it proposed**, whether it presented that date as certain, and
  **your correction** if you made one.
- **Your phone's platform and operating system version** (for example
  "ios 26.0") — because a text recogniser's behaviour depends on the OS it
  shipped in.

### What is never sent

- **The name you typed for the item.** It's your words, it has nothing to do
  with reading the date, and the app removes it from the shared copy.
- Your other items, your reminders, your calendar or your settings.
- Your name, email address, account, phone number or any device identifier.
- Any photograph you didn't report.

### How it is sent

By email, to the developer, **over Wi-Fi only**. Reports wait on your phone
until the phone is on Wi-Fi or Ethernet; they are never sent over mobile data,
and never over a connection the app cannot positively identify as unmetered.

If you switch the setting off, reports that haven't been sent yet are dropped
and never sent.

### "Anonymous" — and what that honestly means

A report carries no name, no account and no device identifier, and nothing in it
says who you are.

It does contain **a photograph you took**, and a photograph can show more than
its subject — your kitchen, your hands, your shopping. That's why the app says
both halves of this in the same breath rather than just the reassuring one.

**About photo metadata:** photographs taken with GroceryDate's own camera carry
no location — the app holds no location permission at all, so there is nothing
for it to record. If you instead choose a photo from **your photo library**, it
may carry whatever metadata your camera saved into it when you took it, which
can include location if you had location services enabled for your camera. If
that matters to you, use the in-app camera for reports, or turn location off for
your camera app.

### What happens to a report afterwards

Reports arrive in the developer's mailbox and are used for one thing: working
out why a label was read wrong, and fixing it. They are not sold, not shared
with anyone else, not used for advertising, and not combined with anything else
about you — there is nothing to combine them with.

A photograph that turns out to be genuinely useful may become a permanent test
case, so that the same misreading can never come back. Where that happens, the
picture is cropped to the label first, and anything personal in the frame —
hands, faces, floors, surroundings — is removed before it is kept.

The copy on your phone is **not** deleted when a report is sent, so you keep
your own copy of everything that went.

## Permissions the app asks for

| Permission | What it's for | Optional? |
|---|---|---|
| Camera | Photographing the date | Needed to take new photos; you can pick from your library instead |
| Photo library | Reading a date from a photo you already have | Yes |
| Calendar | Adding and removing this app's own reminder events | Yes — the app works without it |
| Notifications | Delivering the reminder | Yes, but it's the main feature |
| Network (Android: internet & network state) | **Only** for sending misread reports, and only to check for Wi-Fi | Yes — unused unless you turn sharing on |

GroceryDate does not request location, contacts, microphone, or health data.

## Children

GroceryDate is not directed at children and collects nothing about who is using
it.

## Changes to this policy

If what the app does changes, this page changes with it, and the date at the top
is updated. Anything that would newly send information off your phone would be
off by default and would ask you first — as the existing feature does.

### What is planned, and is not here yet

Everything above describes the app as it is today. Two changes are intended for
a later version, and neither is in this one:

- **Advertising.** A future version is expected to be supported by ads. This
  page will be updated before that version ships, and will say what the ads see.
- **Using your photographs for more than reading the date.** If the pictures you
  take were ever used for anything beyond finding the expiry date and, where you
  opted in, fixing a misreading, that would be a new purpose. It would be
  described here first, it would be **off by default**, and it would ask you
  separately.

Neither of these applies to the version you are using. This section is here so
that you know the direction of travel rather than finding out from a changelog.
