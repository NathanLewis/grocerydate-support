# GroceryDate Support

GroceryDate photographs the expiration date on a food item, reads the date, and
reminds you before it runs out — with both a calendar event and a notification
carrying the photo, so you can see what it is rather than guess from a name.

All scanning happens **on your phone**. Nothing is uploaded.

## Frequently asked questions

### The app read the date wrong. What should I do?

Correct it on the confirmation screen before saving — tap the date and pick the
right one. The reminder is then set from your correction, not from the misread.

If you'd like the misreading fixed for everyone, tap **Report misread date** on
that same screen. The first time, the app asks whether you're happy to send the
photo and the scanned text to the developer; you can decline, and you can change
your mind later in **Settings → Help improve scanning**. Nothing is ever sent
unless you say yes.

### Why does the date need to be the right way up?

The viewfinder scans text as it's printed. A stamp printed sideways or upside
down reads as garbled digits, and a garbled digit can still look like a valid
date — which is worse than reading nothing. Turn the pack so the date reads
normally, and keep it inside the bright box in the middle of the viewfinder.

### Why is there a box in the middle of the camera screen?

That's the area the app reads. Keeping the date inside it stops the ingredients
list, the barcode or a lot code from competing with the date you actually want.
The photo itself is never cropped — the whole picture is saved with the item.

### It says "please check this date". What does that mean?

The app is telling you it isn't certain. That usually means the stamp was
faint, part of it was blurred, or two readings of the same label disagreed by a
digit. Have a glance at the pack: tap **Looks right** to confirm, or **Edit** to
correct it. Both answers clear the warning.

### Why does GroceryDate want my calendar?

So the expiry lands somewhere you already look. Each item adds one event to
your default calendar, and removing the item removes the event again. The app
only writes its own events — it never reads or sends anything else in your
calendar.

If you'd rather not grant calendar access, the app still works: the item is
still tracked and you still get the notification. You just lose the calendar
half.

### Why does the reminder have a picture on it?

Because "yoghurt" three weeks later isn't much help. A calendar can't show an
image, but a notification can, so the notification carries the photo you took.

### When exactly does it remind me?

At **09:00**, one day before the expiry date by default. You can change the
default in **Settings → Reminders → Default days before expiry**, and override
it for a single item on the confirmation screen when you save it.

### Does it work without an internet connection?

Yes, entirely. Reading the date, saving the item, the calendar event and the
reminder all work with no connection at all. The only thing that ever uses the
network is the optional misread reporting described above — and that waits for
Wi-Fi.

### It didn't find a date at all.

Some things to try, roughly in order of how often they help:

- Move closer, so the date fills more of the box.
- Turn the pack so the date reads right way up.
- Get more light on it, or let the flash fire — dot-matrix stamps need it.
- Flatten the label if it's curved or creased.
- Try a slightly different angle; a glossy pack can reflect the light straight
  back into the lens.

If it still won't read, you can always enter the date by hand — the item is
saved either way.

### Can I use a photo I've already taken?

Yes. Choose from your library instead of shooting a new one. The app reads it
the same way.

### What languages does it speak?

English, Italian and Arabic. Set it in **Settings → Language → App language**, or leave it to
follow your phone. Arabic lays the app out right-to-left automatically.

The app also recognises expiry wording in all of those languages when it reads
a label, whatever language you've set — labels travel.

### The camera makes a sound. Can I turn it off?

**Settings → Camera → Shutter sound**, slide it to zero. (iOS only — on Android the
shutter sound belongs to your phone's camera app, not to GroceryDate.)

### What happens when I delete an item?

The photo, the calendar event and the pending reminder all go with it.

### Which phones does it run on?

iPhone on iOS 16 or later, and Android 7.0 (API 24) or later.
